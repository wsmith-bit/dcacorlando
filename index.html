HVAC Repair FAQs for Orlando Residents

What should I do if my air conditioning system stops working in Orlando?

If your air conditioning system stops working, check your thermostat settings first. Ensure that the unit is set to cooling mode and that the temperature is set lower than the current room temperature. If it still doesn’t function, it’s advisable to call a professional HVAC technician who can diagnose issues such as refrigerant leaks or electrical problems, common in Orlando’s humid conditions.

How can I tell if I need to replace my HVAC system instead of repairing it?

Consider replacing your HVAC system if it’s over 15 years old and requires frequent repairs. Additionally, if your energy bills are rising significantly or you notice inconsistent temperatures, these can be signs of a failing system. A replacement might be more cost-effective in the long run, especially for homeowners near Lake Eola, where newer systems can offer better energy efficiency.

What are the average costs for HVAC repairs in Orlando?

The cost of HVAC repairs in Orlando can vary based on the specific service required. For instance, minor repairs like replacing a thermostat might cost less than major repairs involving compressor issues. It’s important to obtain a detailed estimate from a technician, as costs can differ based on the complexity of the problem and the type of system you have.

What maintenance should I perform on my HVAC system to prevent emergencies?

Regular maintenance for your HVAC system includes changing air filters every 1-3 months and scheduling annual inspections with a professional. This can help identify potential problems early and ensure your system operates efficiently. Property managers along International Drive often opt for maintenance contracts to keep their systems in top shape and avoid unexpected breakdowns.

Homeowners in Orlando should also be alert to any sudden increases in energy bills, which can indicate inefficiencies in the system. If you notice any of these symptoms, it's important to address them promptly to avoid more costly repairs later.
